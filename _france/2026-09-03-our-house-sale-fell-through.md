Yeah. Deeply frustrating, non?

We'd been pushing things at our end (as has our buyer) for some weeks, with the delays being at the bottom of our very small chain. These were first time buyers, and their solicitor got wrapped up in a non-issue that ultimately screwed things for us.

To step back in time, briefly.... Once upon a time, Main Road, Tolpuddle had a row of allotments behind about 8 of the houses, abutting the end of their gardens. Long before we arrived, the residents collectively agreed to buy the land so they could expand their gardens. So when we arrived, we had a potted history of the sale and two deeds for the sale (one for the house and old garden, and one for the new land).

So, many purchases (including ours AND our buyer's) have taken place without so much as a raised eyebrow under this arrangement. That is until the first-time buyers' solicitor took a look and decided to take it all the way to the mortgage company for their approval. They decided it was a risk (it isn't) and the proper course of action would be to apply for a change of use for the land from allotment to garden.

Wanna know the real irony here? The mortgage company in question is the same one that our buyer used to purchase the place. Hilarious.

So now the first time buyers have been scared off, have pulled out and our buyer is looking at a minimum 8 week period to have a Change of Use application considered by the council.

All this while we are out of the country, the garden is getting horribly overgrown and we continue to pay Council Tax and Home Insurance. So, we've decided to put it back on the market but also to leave some wriggle room for our buyer to continue the private sale with us.

Watch this space for developments...

EDIT: we just heard from our buyer that she's had second thoughts too, so it's a good thing we've moved on this already.

## Health update
Today I had my long-awaited MRI to help finalise my spondyloarthritis diagnosis. I've had these scans in the past (for my epilepsy) so it was interesting to see how things had changed since my last one.

I was very mindful of my less-than-ideal CT scan appointment that ended up with an ambulance ride and admittance to hospital, but that was at the very low point in my condition. This time I cam prepared with Cushion In A Bag &trade; so I could sit down while waiting. As it happens I only had to wait 2 minutes and I was in!

While the CT scan was 45 minutes of standing in awkward positions while in pain, the MRI was lying down on a cushioned base, and I even had a leg support! So I was perfectly able to stay still for the duration of the scan.

The biggest change since my last MRI was the visual display. Staring up at the ceiling I was greeted by a soothing video image that changed periodically, had a changing background colour and (best part for me) was that each scan run was represented on a divided progress bar so I could see exactly how far through the process was left. I found [a site that shows a similar system in use](https://www.pdcbiz.com/products/mri-in-bore-viewing-video-wireless/.)

I did notice that the birds on one video flew in exactly the same pattern each time it was shown, so I was immediately suspicious and declared "I will not clean!" before they taped me up and pushed me out of the air hatch. (One for the *Silo* fans there).

So, just a week to go before my outpatients appointment with the additional blood test (easy) and Shingles vaccination (horrible) having been undertaken as instructed.

The sheer volume of paperwork from all this is really building up. Fingers crossed that by the next blog I'm on the new injections.

## Tax office visit

I'd been asked by our financial advisers to obtain a French tax ID number as part of their setting up our *Assurance Vie*. They had, offhand, suggested it was as easy as going online (warning: not much in France is really that simple) which we found not to be true, so being the troopers we are we downloaded the forms and completed them by hand.

We also though handing them in to the local tax office would be our best bet to check we had everything correctly completed, so along we went one afternoon. There was a very small queue to get in, and we soon found that it was because someone was physically triaging the forms *outside* the office rather than people being allowed in.

In some ways this mirrored our experience at the visa application office in London, where someone was allocated to check paperwork before letting people in. Not sure if this is a uniquely French practice or not, but it did give us some assurance that we'd got the forms and supporting evidence all correct.

This was followed up by a phone call from the tax office the next day to clarify the nature of our pensions. Rachel was able to explain (in French) that we pay tax on these in the UK only as part of the Dual Taxation Treaty as they are classed as 'Government' pensions. This then led to a rapid conclusion to the process as we weren't eligible to pay any French taxes (yet) so they wouldn't issue a tax ID.

## Our car is nearly French

While we bumble along personally without the much desired *Carte Vitale* (only available to us after 3 months residency) our car has made faster progress towards being fully French. This, however, has been a tortuous process that I have left fully to Rachel!

After obtaining a Certificate of EU Conformity, clearing customs on arrival, getting our duty exemption certificate, undertaking an MOT and applying for our registration plates things moved a little closer to completion last week by our receiving an email to say our application was incomplete.

(Just a note to any French process improvement folk out there; it would help greatly explaining exactly *what* is lacking so appropriate action can be taken. Thank you)

It turned out Rachel's attempt to put things in her name was the root of the issue as the car and its UK registration is in my name, so reprinting/redrafting/resubmitting the forms.

Now we are playing a waiting game with watching *La Poste* to see what the status of the delivery of our licence plates is (currently in Lille, apparently), but soon we should be able to rip off the nasty magnetic 'UK' sticker for good!
